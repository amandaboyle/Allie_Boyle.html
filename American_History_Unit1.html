SECTION 4: Shared Beliefs & Iroquois Council

The Spiritual Web & The Council Fire

Early Americans did not write laws on paper; they shared stories and governed themselves collectively. They believed no person could own land. Instead, it was a sacred, shared trust. Click the cards below to examine their values, then resolve a tribal issue in the **Council Fire Simulator**!

🌱

1. Connection to Land

Click to flip...

Sacred Stewardship

Unlike Europeans, Native Americans **did not believe individuals could own land**. Land was a sacred element of nature, to be shared and protected by all.

💨

2. Spiritual World

Click to flip...

Natural Spirits

They believed the natural world was filled with living spirits (animism). Everything from wind and rain to animals held deep significance and demanded respect.

👥

3. Kinship (Clans)

Click to flip...

Strong Kinship Bonds

Customs focused on family clans. Elders passed down oral histories and rules. Division of labor was balanced: men hunted, women farmed.
